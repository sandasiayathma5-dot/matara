Timeless Matara : A Journey Through Centuries

Matara has a long and valuable history. The city developed through different historical periods and became an important centre for trade, administration and settlement in the southern region.






Portuguese Period

The Portuguese established their influence in the coastal areas of Sri Lanka during the 16th century.Matara became an important location because of its coastal position and strategic value.

Dutch Period

The Dutch later controlled Matara and developed several buildings and defensive structures.Their influence can still be seen in the historic buildings and architectural features of the city.

British Period

During the British period, Matara continued to develop as an administrative and commercial centre. Roads, buildings and other urban facilities were developed during this period.









Matara Fort

Originally built by the Portuguese in 1560, Matara Fort underwent a major rebuild in 1640 after the Dutch captured nearby Galle and took control of the area. Constructed from sturdy granite stone and coral—much like its sibling, the nearby Star Fort—the fort features a massive stone rampart that creates a dramatic natural barrier separating the calm waters of the Nilwala River from the open Indian Ocean.

Star Fort

The Star Fort, located in Matara on the eastern bank of the Nilwala River, is an 18th-century Dutch fortification built between 1763 and 1765. Constructing it following the 1761 Matara Rebellion to protect the main fort from riverborne attacks, the Dutch designed the structure in a unique six-pointed star shape equipped with space for 12 large cannons. Built from granite rock and coral with 14-foot-thick rampart walls, the fort is enclosed by a 6-meter-wide moat and featured internal facilities such as a small garrison, ammunition storage, prison cells, and a central water well. Today, it remains in good condition under the Government of Sri Lanka and is open to the public.






Old Nupe Market

The Old Nupe Market, also known as the Old Dutch Market, is a historic colonial building located in Matara, Sri Lanka. Built in the late 18th century, this T-shaped open-sided market features large white stone pillars, fine wooden lattice work, and a roof tiled with traditional red clay. Historically, it served as a lively trading hub divided into sections for selling fresh vegetables, meat, fish, textiles, and household items. Today, it stands as an important heritage site protected by the Archaeological Department of Sri Lanka.






Dutch Reformed Church

The Dutch Reformed Church in Matara, consecrated in 1706, is one of the oldest active Protestant churches in Sri Lanka. Located inside the Matara Fort, this modest, Doric-style building was constructed during the Dutch colonial era and features round-headed windows, heavy wooden doors, and historic gravestones paved into its floor. Designed as an elegant structure to seat around 200 people, the church survived damage from the 2004 tsunami, underwent careful restoration, and continues to hold religious services today as a protected heritage site.





Why Should We Protect Heritage?

Historical buildings and places help us understand the identity and development of Matara. Protecting these places is important for future generations and for cultural tourism.
